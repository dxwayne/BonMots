\section{Reduce A Night of Spectra}

The night usually consists of focus images, zeros, darks -- where applicable, flats
and sequences around a target. The files that come from the science computer will
be lacking some keywords, and may require changing others.

For spectra it is critical to set the \gls{dispaxis} right. DISPAXIS=1 means the
dispersion follows rows, where DISPAXIS=2 means it follows columns. 

Zeros, darks and flats are combined using the ``median'' combine
method. Median combining takes the middle-most value from an array. This
avoids cosmic rays hits as they are shoved to one end of that array!
Three files are made: \dhl{zeromaster.fits}, \dhl{darkmaster.fits}, and
\dhl{flatmaster.fits}. Note lots of people use masterxxxx, but tab completion
is easier by putting the type first. z\mousebox{TAB} gets the job done.

Zero exposures are supposed to measure predominatly Gaussian noise associated
with the manufacturing of the chip.  Dark and Light exposures involve Poission
statistics -- related to the arrival rate and spacing of photons. Therefore
it is necessary to take the zeromaster from each dark image prior to making
the darkmaster.

If you insist on averaging, use lots of images. Averaging adds any
cosmic-ray noise. Most cosmic ray removal algorithms get most, but not
always all of the noise. Note: CMOS images have some issues. See
\ref{section:CMOSChips} for details.

A \dhl{sequence} consists of a subbset of files for the target star and a subset
of files for the reference star. Slewing the telescope changes the gravity
vector through the instrument distorting images by a a pixel or two. This
amounts to several angstroms of error for small telescopes.

The flat is \dhl{NOT TREATED LIKE PHOTOMETRIC} flats. More on that in
\ref{section:SpectrumFlatFields}

\subsection{Background Fit}

The background is the area just above and just below the trace
where the sky background is extracted. With spectrographs with
extreme smile (curved nature of the spectral lines, evicenced
by the calibration image, is pronounced) blurs the sky contamination.
Keep the area about the same as the combined distance between u-l
used to set the trace's width. 

\llbox{z}-\llbox{z} - remove the two segments \\
\llbox{s}-\llbox{s} - set the left segment \\
\llbox{s}-\llbox{s} - set the right segment \\
\llbox{f} - fit for background \\
